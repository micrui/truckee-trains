The train walk

Truckee grew up around the railroad, and most of what that history left behind can still be seen in a single day. Five stops downtown sit within a short walk of each other. The summit tunnels and four regional museums fill out the rest.

Downtown, on foot

  1. The depot, 10065 Donner Pass Road. Southern Pacific built it in 1900 from local pine after the original 1869-era depot burned, and Nevada County bought it in 1985. Today it holds the Amtrak waiting room, the Truckee Chamber's visitor center, the Museum of Truckee History at the east end, and the Truckee Donner Railroad Society's caboose museum at the west end.
  2. The Bridge Street crossing. Just east of the depot, this is the only place in town where a public road crosses the main tracks at grade, meaning at street level. This site's crossing page covers its record.
  3. The Truckee Railyard display. A short walk east of Commercial Row, beside the main line, stands Southern Pacific rotary snowplow 210, built by Alco in 1937, which cleared this line's snow for decades; the California State Railroad Museum donated it in 2007. Beside it is the 1930 steam wreck derrick 7050, a crane used to clear wrecks on the Donner line.
  4. The Chinatown memorial plaque, Jibboom Street. The plaque, in front of the Old Jail Museum, was dedicated May 10, 2024. Truckee's first Chinatown grew along Jibboom Street from 1867, serving the subcontractors who worked on the railroad; this site's labor page carries that history.
  5. The Old Jail Museum, 10142 Jibboom Street. Built in 1875 at the corner of Spring and Jibboom, it is run by the Truckee-Donner Historical Society. It is open summer weekends, 10 to 4, staffing permitting.

One more site has nothing left to see. The railroad yards east of downtown once held a 22-stall granite roundhouse, a building where locomotives were serviced, built in 1882 and demolished in 1955. In the 1870s Truckee shipped more freight than any other point on the Central Pacific. No marker stands at the site.

Up the pass

The original 1868 grade over Donner Summit, including the hand-drilled Summit Tunnel (Tunnel 6, 1,659 feet long) and the China Wall retaining wall, was taken out of service in 1993 and the track removed. Walkers reach the area from Donner Pass Road near the Rainbow Bridge; the tunnels are under a quarter mile from the road. In 2021 the National Trust for Historic Preservation put the tunnels and the workers' camp beside them on its list of America's most endangered historic places. For roadside history, the Donner Summit Historical Society's 20-Mile Museum is a series of interpretive signs along old Highway 40 from Cisco Grove over the summit, covering the tunnels, snowsheds, the China Wall, and more. It is free and drivable when the road is open; Donner Pass Road over the summit closes in storms, so check road conditions in winter. The society publishes a sign-by-sign guide, maps, a route brochure, and a photo gallery of Tunnel 6 as it looks today.

Access status: the grade and tunnels remain Union Pacific private property, posted against trespassing, and are also widely walked and openly described by local visitor guides; there is no formal public access. Union Pacific said in 2021 that it still uses the area for staging equipment and is open to proposals honoring the site's history.

Day trips

Western Pacific Railroad Museum, Portola. About an hour north via SR 89 and SR 70, at 700 Western Pacific Way, it holds one of the largest diesel locomotive collections in the country, offers caboose rides, and runs a program that lets a visitor operate a locomotive with an engineer aboard.

California State Railroad Museum, Old Sacramento. At 111 I Street, open daily 10 to 5, it holds cab-forward 4294, the only survivor of the backwards-built steam locomotives that worked this pass.

Nevada State Railroad Museum, Carson City. At 2180 S. Carson Street, open Thursday to Monday, it holds 65 locomotives and cars, 31 of them from the Virginia & Truckee.

Donner Memorial State Park visitor center, Truckee. Open daily 10 to 5, it includes an exhibit on the roughly 12,000 Chinese workers who built the railroad over the pass.

Sources: Western Pacific Railroad Museum · California State Railroad Museum · Nevada State Railroad Museum · Donner Memorial State Park
