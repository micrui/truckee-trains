The Bridge Street crossing

Bridge Street is the only place in Truckee where a public road crosses the through tracks at grade, the only such crossing for about eight miles in either direction, and by far the busiest on the mountain. Every horn heard downtown is sounded here, because federal regulation requires it.

For comparison, the nearest main-line grade crossings are Stampede Meadows Road, eight miles east near Boca (1,193 vehicles a day), and Old Donner Summit Road, fourteen miles west at Norden (30 vehicles a day). Every other road for the length of the mountain passes over or under the tracks. The three other gated crossings inside Truckee (Donner Pass Road and two on Church Street) reach only yard tracks, with zero through trains in the federal record. This site pulls the Bridge Street record monthly and logs any change.

Source: FRA National Highway-Rail Crossing Inventory, records retrievable by the crossing IDs above; the stored copy of the Bridge Street record and its change log are in the repository.

Why the horns sound

Federal regulation requires every train to sound its horn on approach to every public grade crossing: beginning 15 to 20 seconds out, in the pattern two longs, a short, and a long, held until the locomotive occupies the crossing. The same rules set the horn's loudness: no quieter than 96 and no louder than 110 decibels, measured 100 feet ahead of the locomotive. The engineer has no discretion to skip it, and the railroad no authority to quiet it. About fifteen times a day, seven of them at night, that sequence sounds at Bridge Street. (One narrow exception: a train stopped close to a crossing may sound less than the full warning.)

Sources: 49 CFR § 222.21 · 49 CFR § 229.129

The accident record

The federal accident file for Bridge Street holds four incidents: January 1979, May 1981, February 1985, and June 1991. All four involved automobiles, none injured anyone, property damage ran $500 to $1,000, and all four occurred in the Southern Pacific era. In the thirty-five years since 1991, no accident at this crossing has been reported to the FRA. Stampede Meadows Road, with a fraction of the traffic, has seven on file through 2021.

Source: FRA crossing accident reports (Form 6180.57), file current to May 2026.

The quiet zone project

The horn can be silenced only one way: the Town can establish a quiet zone under the same federal rule, by upgrading the crossing until its gates and engineering provide the warning the horn otherwise provides. The Town is pursuing exactly that. Its Reimagine Bridge Street project lists designating a quiet zone as an explicit goal, alongside new vehicle and pedestrian gates, a rebuilt crossing surface, and new signals at Bridge and Donner Pass Road and Bridge and River Street. The council authorized grant applications toward the roughly $6.3 million project in August 2022 and awarded a design contract in January 2025. The quiet zone's own cost and timeline are not yet public. This page tracks the project's record as it moves; when the crossing's soundscape changes, the planned sensor log will have measured it before and after.
