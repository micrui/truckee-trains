What the line carries

Two ways over the same mountain: about fifteen freight trains a day on the rails, about six and a half thousand trucks a day on the freeway beside them. This page keeps the comparable facts in one place, including the studies that have never been done.

The rail line has carried double-stacked containers since Union Pacific's 2009 tunnel clearance project, which removed the last physical reason intermodal freight had to detour around the Sierra. What share of the corridor's freight moves by each mode is not published anywhere; the railroad releases no tonnage figures for the pass.

Roughly forty people a day, on average, board or leave the Zephyr in Truckee in a normal year: a small number beside the freeway's, carried on two trains that also serve every town between the Bay and Chicago. How reliably those two trains run is on the status page, from this site's own log.

What has been studied, and what has not

The California State Rail Plan's vision reaches Reno from the Sacramento Valley by connecting bus, not by train. The Capitol Corridor has long studied extending service over this line to Reno; in December 2023 the corridor, with a Reno/Sparks extension in its scope, entered the federal Corridor Identification planning program, though no service is designed or funded. No public agency has published a study of diverting I-80 truck freight to rail over Donner, and no modern proposal exists to electrify the line. Whether any of those should exist is a policy question; what exists in the public record today is the list above.
