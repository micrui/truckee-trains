How the railroad got here

The line through Truckee is the original transcontinental crossing of the Sierra, and it has been rebuilt underneath the trains for a century and a half. A dated record; who did the building is its own page.

1863–68

The Central Pacific builds east from Sacramento into the mountains. The Summit Tunnel at Donner Pass, hand-drilled through granite from four faces, opens November 30, 1867; track reaches the Truckee River canyon and the settlement that becomes Truckee in 1868, and the town grows up around the railroad's yards, ice houses, and mills.

Pacific Southwest Railway Historical Society timeline · Truckee-Donner Historical Society

1869

The golden spike at Promontory joins the Central Pacific and Union Pacific on May 10. Donner Pass becomes the mountain gateway of the first transcontinental railroad, and everything crossing between Northern California and the East passes through Truckee.

Golden Spike National Historical Park

1860s–70s

The railroad answers the Sierra winter with wooden snowsheds, eventually roofing tens of miles of mountain track. The sheds, endlessly rebuilt after fires and storms, are what made year-round operation over the pass possible at all.

Donner Summit Historical Society, Heirloom archives

1901–10

The Southern Pacific era brings the line's signature machines: surveys for a long summit tunnel and even electrification are studied and shelved, and from 1910 the cab-forward steam locomotives, built backward so crews would not suffocate in the snowsheds, take over the mountain.

PSRHS timeline

1925

A second track over the summit opens, carried through the two-mile Tunnel 41 under Mount Judah. The mountain now runs as a double-track railroad: the 1868 line over the top, the 1925 line through the mountain.

Tunnel 41 · PSRHS timeline

1958

Steam ends on Donner. The last cab-forward crossed in 1957, the final steam excursion in October 1958; diesels have run the pass ever since. The sole surviving cab-forward stands in the California State Railroad Museum.

California State Railroad Museum · PSRHS timeline

1983

Amtrak's train over Donner, until then the San Francisco Zephyr, is renamed the California Zephyr when its mid-route alignment shifts to Colorado; the Donner crossing itself has hosted passenger trains continuously since 1868. The Zephyr still stops in Truckee twice a day, westbound late morning and eastbound early afternoon.

San Francisco Zephyr · California Zephyr · this site's schedule log

1993

The railroad singles the summit: the original 1868 track over the top of the pass, including the hand-cut Summit Tunnel, is taken out of service, and all traffic moves to the 1925 alignment through Tunnel 41. The abandoned tunnels become one of the Sierra's most visited hikes.

Abandoned Rails, Donner Pass

1996

Union Pacific absorbs the Southern Pacific, and the line becomes part of the UP system it remains in today, dispatched as the Roseville Subdivision.

Union Pacific history · FRA inventory (subdivision name)

2009

Union Pacific notches the crowns of fifteen tunnels between Rocklin and Truckee, more than 18,000 feet of cutting, to clear double-stacked containers over Donner for the first time. The project quietly re-made the line's economics: a modern intermodal train could now take the short mountain route instead of detouring.

Union Pacific release, Nov. 23, 2009

Today

The federal crossing record puts about fifteen through trains a day at Truckee, eight by day and seven by night, including the two Zephyrs. The railroad publishes no schedule and no tonnage figures for the pass; what can be measured from town, this site is building the tools to measure.
